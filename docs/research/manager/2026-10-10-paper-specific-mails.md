Subject: TAME: testing agency after a change to shared conditions

Dear Professor Levin,

I’m Daisuke Narita, based in Tokyo. My master’s research at NAIST/ATR studied EEG-based mind-wandering detection and generalization across participants. I now work on deployed enterprise AI agents.

I’m designing Manager: AI that changes shared resource access and institutional conditions, then measures outcomes for people who never use it. The architecture and evaluation design are in progress; I do not yet have field results.

TAME’s cognitive light cone distinguishes an agent’s area of concern from its sensing or effector range. I want to test a related distinction: if shared infrastructure improves group performance while individual capabilities stay fixed, what experiment would distinguish better coordination from a larger, goal-directed collective agent? Would adaptation after perturbing the shared infrastructure be an informative test?

If this connects with your research, I’d appreciate a brief reply or a 20-minute conversation. I can send a one-page experiment outline.

Best,
Daisuke Narita

Subject: From individual simulations to structural interventions

Dear Professor Bernstein,

I’m Daisuke Narita, based in Tokyo. My master’s research at NAIST/ATR studied EEG-based mind-wandering detection and generalization across participants. I now work on deployed enterprise AI agents.

I’m designing Manager, an AI system that would change shared supply, access, or rules and evaluate benefits to people who never use an app.

Your individual-simulation work grounds agents in interviews and surveys and evaluates held-out responses relative to participants’ test-retest consistency. That seems a useful starting point, but our intended interventions change what actions are feasible, not only what respondents are asked.

What prospective validation would you require before using such agents to rank changes to a service network or allocation rule? In particular, how would you detect a policy ranking that changes across plausible response models despite good held-out survey accuracy?

I’d welcome brief feedback or a 20-minute conversation. I can share a one-page evaluation design; we do not yet have field results.

Best,
Daisuke Narita

Subject: Individuality and the scale of a shared-system intervention

Dear Professor Krakauer,

I’m Daisuke Narita, based in Tokyo. My master’s research at NAIST/ATR studied EEG-based mind-wandering detection and generalization across participants. I now work on deployed enterprise AI agents.

I’m designing Manager: AI that changes shared infrastructure or institutional conditions and measures the effects on people who never interact with it.

The Information Theory of Individuality treats coordinated spatial and temporal processes as a basis for identifying individuals, rather than assuming a boundary in advance. For an intervention that improves outcomes across a population, I want to avoid calling any increase in coordination a new collective intelligence.

What information-theoretic measurements and perturbations would you use to distinguish improved individual performance from a new, persistent system-level capacity? How would you choose the boundary and time scale without selecting them to fit the desired result?

Would you be open to brief feedback or a 20-minute research conversation? I can send a one-page experiment outline. The work is currently at the design stage.

Best,
Daisuke Narita
