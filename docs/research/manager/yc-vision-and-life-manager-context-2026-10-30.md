### The loop

1. Observe real signals and identify a question about behavior.
2. Fit or reuse a model that predicts the distribution of reactions, with uncertainty.
3. Simulate different feasible, permitted changes quickly; include doing nothing.
4. Select a limited action under legitimate user goals, costs, consent, and harm limits.
5. Have one authorized Life Manager runtime/agent execute it, with deduplication.
6. Check real provider receipts and observed behavior/economics/welfare.
7. Compare against an honest baseline; preserve failures; update memory, model or policy only after independent evaluation.

**Critical research distinction:** Prediction accuracy is not causal intervention accuracy, simulator performance is not real-world performance, and increased sales are not proof of reduced suffering. The simulator's uncertainty and the gap between synthetic and human responses are central problems to solve.

## Scientific positioning and what to learn before reinventing

- **Behavior simulation and human models:** Artificial Societies / Rothko, Simile, Aaru, Listen Twins, humans& Persimmon; examine data rights, heldout people/time, calibration, distribution fidelity, and limitations.
- **Simulation-trained policy:** humans& Ampersand, model-based RL, existing social simulators including Concordia and relevant agent benchmarks; the simulator is not proof of human welfare gains.
- **Causal intervention:** established trials, public intervention-effect datasets, JITAI/MRT, causal inference and experimental design.
- **Real-world action and economic evaluations:** the Listen Labs / StyleFits closed-loop example, Andon Labs Vending-Bench-to-real-world work, comparable real-world agent evaluations.
- **Decision representation and state:** Palantir-style ontology and controlled what-if planning, without confusing ontology with omniscience.

**Benchmark aim:** First reuse existing public evaluation code and real observation-based baselines. Then measure (1) human response prediction, (2) intervention-effect accuracy, (3) decision quality and incremental value over no simulation, (4) execution reliability, (5) net economics, (6) human well-being and adverse effects, and (7) transfer to unseen domains. Preregister predictions and keep test data independent. Model size, simulated population counts, and virtual earnings do not themselves establish general intelligence.
