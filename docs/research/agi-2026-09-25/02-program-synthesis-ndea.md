## 1. "On the Measure of Intelligence" (arXiv:1911.01547, Chollet, Google Inc., Nov 2019)

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01547 and full text https://arxiv.org/html/1911.01547v2

- Core thesis (abstract, verbatim): "We then articulate a new formal definition of intelligence based on Algorithmic Information Theory, describing intelligence as skill-acquisition efficiency and highlighting the concepts of scope, generalization difficulty, priors, and experience."
- Skill vs. intelligence (verbatim, abstract): "solely measuring skill at any given task falls short of measuring intelligence, because skill is heavily modulated by prior knowledge and experience: unlimited priors or unlimited training data allow experimenters to 'buy' arbitrary levels of skills for a system, in a way that masks the system's own generalization power."
- ARC's purpose (verbatim, abstract): "we present a benchmark closely following these guidelines, the Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus (ARC), built upon an explicit set of priors designed to be as close as possible to innate human priors... ARC can be used to measure a human-like form of general fluid intelligence."
- Core Knowledge priors — four systems used to define ARC's fair scope (verbatim, full text):
  1. **Objectness and elementary physics**: "humans assume that their environment should be parsed into 'objects' characterized by principles of cohesion (objects move as continuous, connected, bounded wholes), persistence (objects do not suddenly cease to exist and do not suddenly materialize), and contact (objects do not act at a distance and cannot interpenetrate)."
  2. **Agentness and goal-directedness**: "humans assume that, while some objects in their environment are inanimate, some other objects are 'agents', possessing intentions of their own, acting so as to achieve goals... and showing efficiency in their goal-directed actions."
  3. **Numbers and counting priors**: "Many ARC tasks involve counting or sorting objects..., comparing numbers..., or repeating a pattern for a fixed number of time. The notions of addition and subtraction are also..." part of this prior.
  4. **Elementary geometry and topology**: "captures notions of distance, orientation, in/out relationships for objects in our environment and for ourselves. It underlies humans' innate facility for orienting themselves with respect to their surroundings and navigating 2D and 3D environments."
  - Origin of these priors: "Core Knowledge has been written into our DNA by natural evolution... it is evolutionarily ancient and largely shared across many species, in particular non-human primates."
- Note: the paper frames these as the *only* priors an ARC-fair test should assume, i.e. no acquired/cultural knowledge (no English, no specific facts) — this is why ARC grids use small integers/colors rather than language.

## 3. Chollet's 2024-2026 talks/essays on fluid intelligence, test-time adaptation, abstraction, kaleidoscope hypothesis

Primary source successfully retrieved: **arcprize.org/blog/beat-arc-agi-deep-learning-and-program-synthesis** (28 Oct 2024, Knoop + Chollet, ARC Prize University Tour talk transcript) — https://arcprize.org/blog/beat-arc-agi-deep-learning-and-program-synthesis

Key verbatim claims from Chollet's section of that talk:
- **Skill vs. fluid intelligence**: "the distinction between static skills and fluid intelligence, between having access to a collection of static programs to solve known problems, versus being able to synthesize brand new programs on the fly to face a problem you've never seen before."
- **LLMs as memorized-program lookup**: "My hypothesis is that LLM performance depends purely on task familiarity. You can make a LLM do anything as long as you give it the opportunity to memorize the solution. But it cannot adapt to the slightest amount of novelty."
- **Two forms of abstraction (value-centric vs. program-centric)**: "There are 2 key categories of analogies from which arise 2 categories of abstraction: value-centric abstraction, and program-centric abstraction... Value-centric abstraction is about comparing things via a continuous distance function. It's what powers human perception, intuition, pattern recognition... program-centric abstraction is about comparing discrete programs... rather than computing distances you're looking for exact subgraph isomorphisms... it's what software engineers do." Mapped to System 1 / System 2 (Kahneman) and left-brain/right-brain as metaphor only ("This is a metaphor, mind you. This is not literally how the brain works.").
- **Path forward = combine both**: "The key system 2 technique is discrete search over a space of programs. The blocker... is combinatorial explosion. Meanwhile, the key system 1 technique is curve fitting and interpolation... The big idea is to leverage those fast, but approximate judgement calls to fight combinatorial explosion... as a form of intuition about the structure of the program space."
- **"Kaleidoscope hypothesis"**: NOT found verbatim in any source retrieved this session (not on this blog post, not surfaced via crwl on ndea.com/arcprize.org blog index). UNVERIFIED / not located — general web search was blocked (see Method Notes below); this term is known publicly to be associated with Chollet's writing on reusable "kaleidoscope" abstractions in program synthesis, but I could not retrieve a primary-source quote this session. Flag as unverified rather than guess at wording.
- **Test-time adaptation**: not a standalone essay retrieved this session, but the concept is directly discussed in the same talk via the o1 test-time-compute analysis and echoed in the HRM/TRM/marc repos below (all of which are literally about test-time adaptation/training). No single 2025/2026 Chollet essay titled around "test-time adaptation" was located; ARC-AGI-3 launch post (below) is the most recent primary essay found.
- **ARC-AGI-3 launch** (25 Mar 2026): https://arcprize.org/blog/arc-agi-3-launch — verbatim: "ARC-AGI-3 is the first fully interactive benchmark in the ARC-AGI series... To succeed, an AI agent must explore each environment on its own, figure out how it works, discover what winning looks like, and carry what it learns forward across increasingly difficult levels. Humans score 100%. Frontier AI scores 0.51%." Launch event featured "a fireside conversation between François Chollet (creator, ARC-AGI) and Sam Altman (CEO, OpenAI) on measuring intelligence on the path to AGI" (25 Mar 2026, YC HQ). ## 4. Program-synthesis / neurosymbolic lineage — mechanisms, scores, repos

| System | Mechanism (2 lines) | ARC score | Repo |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DreamCoder** (Ellis et al.) | Wake-sleep program induction: "wake" phase searches for programs solving tasks using a learned neural recognition model; "sleep" phase compresses solved programs into a growing library (DSL) via abstraction, and trains the recognition model on replays. Canonical library-learning system; not ARC-specific originally. | N/A (not ARC's original benchmark); an ARC adaptation exists as a separate repo. | `ellisk42/ec` — **skipped, not cloned**: repo size 3.76 GB via `gh api repos/ellisk42/ec --jq .size` (3,760,739 KB), over the 300MB per-repo cap. |
| DreamCoder-for-ARC (community) | Same wake/sleep/library-learning loop as DreamCoder, applied to ARC-AGI tasks with an ARC-specific DSL. | UNVERIFIED score (not measured this session) | `mxbi/dreamcoder-arc` (338 MB via `gh api` size=346045 KB) — found but not cloned (budget prioritized to 8 smaller/more central repos). |
| **LILO** (Grand et al., "Learning Interpretable Libraries by Compressing and Documenting Code", arxiv/2310.19791) | Extends DreamCoder: LLM proposes/writes candidate programs (replacing pure enumerative search), Stitch (below) performs fast library abstraction, and an LLM auto-documents each learned library function in natural language for interpretability. | Not ARC-focused per se (general program-induction domains incl. compositional graphics, list ops); no ARC number located this session. | Cloned to `repos/lilo` (~673 MB on disk after depth-1 clone incl. submodules; `gh api` reported size 218975 KB). https://github.com/gabegrand/lilo |
| **Stitch** (Bowers et al., POPL 2023, "Top-Down Synthesis For Library Learning") | Fast, scalable top-down search algorithm for abstraction/library learning over a corpus of programs — the compression engine DreamCoder/LILO rely on for finding reusable subprograms, orders of magnitude faster than DreamCoder's original compressor. Rust core + Python bindings. | N/A (a compression tool, not a solver) | Cloned to `repos/stitch` (93 MB). https://github.com/mlb2251/stitch |
| **Hypothesis search / LLM program synthesis for ARC (Ryan Greenblatt)** | GPT-4o generates k≈2,048 candidate Python solution-programs per task from the few demonstration examples; each candidate is deterministically executed and verified against the demo pairs; log-linear accuracy-vs-k scaling observed. | 43% on ARC-AGI-Pub public leaderboard (per arcprize.org blog, Oct 2024) | Cloned to `repos/arc_draw_more_samples_pub` (1.7 MB). https://github.com/rgreenblatt/arc_draw_more_samples_pub |
| **Jeremy Berman — evolutionary test-time compute** | Uses Claude 3.5 Sonnet to generate candidate solution programs, then runs an evolutionary loop (mutate/select programs based on execution feedback against demo pairs) instead of one-shot sampling — improves on Greenblatt's sampling approach. | 53.6% on ARC-AGI-Pub public leaderboard (record at time of posting, per repo README) | Cloned to `repos/arc_agi` (19 MB). https://github.com/jerber/arc_agi |
| **BARC** ("Bootstrapping ARC" / Li, Ellis et al., "Combining Induction and Transduction for Abstract Reasoning", arXiv:2411.02272) | Generates a large synthetic ARC-style dataset (input/output grids + Python transformation code) from ~162 hand-written seed problems, then trains/fine-tunes models with **both** an induction head (predict the program) and a transduction head (predict the output grids directly), ensembling both predictions. Won 2024 ARC Prize paper track. | Not restated in README header (paper reports SOTA-competitive combined induction+transduction scores circa late 2024); exact number UNVERIFIED this session. | Cloned to `repos/BARC` (71 MB). https://github.com/xu3kev/BARC |
| **Test-time training (MIT, Akyürek et al.), "marc"** | At inference, fine-tunes the model itself on the small number of demonstration pairs of the specific task (parameter updates, not just prompting) before predicting the test output — treats each ARC task as its own tiny training problem ("test-time training", TTT). Paper: "The Surprising Effectiveness of Test-Time Training for Abstract Reasoning." | UNVERIFIED exact number this session (paper reports large gains over baseline w/ TTT; not re-derived from README this session). | Cloned to `repos/marc` (480 KB). https://github.com/ekinakyurek/marc |
| **HRM — Hierarchical Reasoning Model** (Sapient, Singapore) | 27M-param recurrent architecture with two coupled modules: slow "H" (planning) and fast "L" (detail), sharing hidden state across an outer "refinement" loop with a learned halt signal (adaptive compute); trained directly on ARC tasks (no LLM/pretraining) with heavy data augmentation. | Claimed 41% ARC-AGI-1 (public eval); **ARC Prize's own independent re-verification**: 32% ARC-AGI-1 semi-private, 2% ARC-AGI-2 semi-private (arcprize.org/blog/hrm-analysis, 15 Aug 2025). ARC Prize's ablations found the "hierarchical" architecture itself contributes little vs. a plain transformer of the same size — most of the gain comes from the outer refinement loop + task augmentation, and much of the performance is closer to memorizing the eval tasks than general cross-task transfer. | Cloned to `repos/HRM` (540 KB). https://github.com/sapientinc/HRM |
| **TRM — Tiny Recursion Model** (Jolicoeur-Martineau, Samsung SAIL Montreal, "Less is More: Recursive Reasoning with Tiny Networks", arXiv:2510.04871) | Simplifies HRM: a single tiny (~7M param) network recursively updates its own latent state and its draft answer over many steps (no separate H/L modules), explicitly positioned as showing "less is more" — no need for large pretrained foundation models. Repo now archived/read-only by authors. | 45% ARC-AGI-1, 8% ARC-AGI-2 (per repo README, self-reported, not independently re-verified by ARC Prize in sources retrieved this session). | Cloned to `repos/TinyRecursiveModels` (12 MB). https://github.com/SamsungSAILMontreal/TinyRecursiveModels |
