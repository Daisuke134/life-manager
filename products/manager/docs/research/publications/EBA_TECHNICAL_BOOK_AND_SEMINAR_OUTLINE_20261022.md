# EBA / Manager — proposed open technical book and world-seminar program

Working book title: **Evaluating Beneficial AI: From Predictive Models to Verified Improvements in Human Life**.
Not a finished book or peer-reviewed study. Do not imply an invited talk or journal acceptance before it happens.

## Part I: What counts as improvement?
1. Why fluency is not enough: differences between task correctness, behavior fidelity and welfare.
2. Measurement theory of suffering, validated domain outcomes and why a universal scalar is misleading.
3. Statistical inference: probability, calibration, Brier, TVD, divergence, uncertainty, missingness.
4. Potential outcomes, DAGs, ATE/CATE, RCTs/MRTs, causal transport and interference.
5. Distinguishing a good intervention from opt-in persuasion, better infrastructure and NO_ACTION.

## Part II: Learning from the frontier
6. Anthropic: production evals, graders, failure taxonomies, harnesses and hillclimb overfitting.
7. Hamel Husain: judge construction, precision/recall tradeoffs, threshold calibration, cost frontiers.
8. Browser Use/METR/Inspect AI: realistic environments, fixed gold, rewards, leak audits and agent traces.
9. Simile and Aaru: heldout prediction of humans, TVD, survey repeat, uncertainty model.
10. DeepMind Concordia and open behavioral science; descriptive-to-causal pitfalls.

## Part III: Engineering EBA
11. Task schemas, versioning, sources and legal data rights.
12. Reuse existing Agent SDK, Inspect, GrowthBook, DoWhy/EconML, not a new evaluator for its own sake.
13. Four benchmark sub-suites: forecast, intervention, agent/effect and welfare.
14. Building realistic benchmarks from internet cases without future labels or group leaks.
15. Hard safety gates and human-reviewed text graders, counterfactual and no-action baselines.
16. Hidden evaluation and responsible public release: public synthetic test versus sealed field evidence.
17. Reproducible scorecards and uncertainty, cost and compute accounting.
18. Safe recursive optimization: independent holdouts, rollback, variance and reward hacking.

## Part IV: Field science and public accountability
19. Service navigation: the first bounded owned-environment publication.
20. Consent, standing authority, harm monitoring and stopping rules.
21. Experimental financial/mental/physical/environment outcomes and negative spillovers.
22. Replication across people, places and time; from local estimated effects to bounded claims.
23. Open datasets, benchmark stewardship, scientific papers and civil-society review.
24. Why "all suffering has ended" cannot be empirically certified from a finite universe observation.

## Audience adaptations (not invitations already obtained)
- Graduate research lab 45-min technical tutorial + 15-min Q&A: equations, leakage failures, holdout design, live EBA-v0 CLI.
- Applied ML team 30-min demo + 30-min workshop: implement a new fixture and construct a valid, locked grader.
- Startup founders 25-min case study: what to measure before an agent changes the world, why synthetic reward is insufficient.
- Public policy / health 45-min discussion: legitimacy, distributional harm, fairness, consent, auditability.

## Concrete production checklist
- Publish article and repository baseline as an open proposal.
- Open issues for community benchmark design and independent rights review.
- Propose talks to former research labs only with verified contacts and no assumed acceptance.
- Expand book chapters with readable figures, worked equations, runnable source code, proper licenses.
- Prepare primary-source citation register with per-claim paper page IDs and contradictory/negative studies.
- Do not announce experimental outcomes until the evidence packet exists.
