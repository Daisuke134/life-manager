# Verified Demonstration — YouTube Script Writer

## Offline selection notes

This is a repository-owned, offline demonstration of the catalog contract. No YouTube
channel, analytics account, external source, or current market data was accessed. The
facts below are the buyer-supplied brief for this example, not an external claim.

## Concrete input

```text
Topic: Why monthly revenue is not the same as profit.
Audience: Founders building a small online product business.
Length: About three minutes.
Tone: Direct, practical, and calm.
Supplied facts: Revenue, model cost, and payout status are separate fields. A sale can
be recorded while its payout is still pending. No statistics, quotations, or external
sources were supplied.
Goal: Help viewers keep revenue, cost, and payout as separate lines in a monthly review.
CTA: Invite viewers to subscribe for more practical operating-finance explainers.
```

## Actual output

### Title options and thumbnail

1. Revenue Is Not Profit: The Three Lines Every Founder Should Track
2. You Made a Sale — So Why Isn’t It Cash Yet?
3. Stop Calling Revenue Profit

**Thumbnail text:** Revenue ≠ Profit

### Three hook options

1. “A sale can be real, the revenue can be recorded, and the money can still be pending.
   Here is the simple separation that keeps those three facts from getting mixed up.”
2. “If your monthly review has one number called ‘profit,’ pause. Revenue, model cost, and
   payout status answer different questions.”
3. “The fastest way to make a small business report confusing is to combine sales, cost,
   and cash. Let’s split them into three lines.”

### Timestamped script

**0:00–0:20 — Hook**

“A sale can be real, revenue can be recorded, and the payout can still be pending. Those
are not contradictory statements. They are three different lines in the review.”

[B-ROLL: Three labeled cards appear: Revenue / Model cost / Payout status]
[ON-SCREEN: Revenue ≠ Profit ≠ Cash received]

**0:20–0:55 — Name the problem**

“Founders often ask one question: ‘How much did we make?’ But that question hides three
questions. How much did customers pay? What did it cost to produce the work? And has the
money actually become available to withdraw? If the review does not separate those, the
bottom line is hard to interpret.”

[B-ROLL: One vague total splits into three columns]

**0:55–1:35 — Line one: revenue**

“Start with revenue. This is the sales line: what the seller or payment source says was
earned in the period you are measuring. Record the window and the source beside the
number. Do not silently turn a sales total into a profit number.”

[ON-SCREEN: Revenue — source + measurement window]

**1:35–2:10 — Line two: model cost**

“Next, record model cost separately. If the product used a model or another paid service,
that cost belongs beside the revenue, not inside an unexplained adjustment. If the actual
bill or allocation is missing, label the cost unknown. A zero is not proof that the cost
was zero.”

[B-ROLL: A cost line changes from “unknown” to a verified receipt]
[ON-SCREEN: Missing cost = unknown, not $0]

**2:10–2:40 — Line three: payout status**

“Finally, track payout status. A recorded sale may be confirmed, pending, payable, or
already paid out. That status tells you about cash availability; it does not rewrite the
revenue or cost line.”

[ON-SCREEN: Revenue / Cost / Payout status — keep separate]

**2:40–3:00 — Review routine and CTA**

“At month end, write the three lines, attach the source and window to each one, and only
call the remainder profit when the cost evidence is complete. If you want more practical
operating-finance explainers, subscribe. In the next video, we’ll build a one-page review
that keeps these lines visible.”

[B-ROLL: A one-page monthly review fills with the three lines]
[ON-SCREEN: Source • Window • Evidence status]
