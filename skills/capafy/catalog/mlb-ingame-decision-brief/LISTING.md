MLB In-Game Decision Brief

## shortDescription
Paste one MLB game moment, decision question, and availability notes for an evidence-bound decision card, conditional paths, and coverage line. It analyzes only what you supply.

## detailedDescription
✨ What you get

- An evidence-bound decision card that separates the supplied game state from interpretation
- Up to three conditional paths with trade-offs and the fact that would change each read
- A concise coverage line and verification queue for a broadcast, article, or informed fan discussion

⚙️ How it works

1. Paste one game's inning, score, base/out state, decision question, and the bullpen, bench, or matchup notes you want considered.
2. I organize the supplied record into documented constraints, cautious interpretations, and conditional paths.
3. You get a self-contained text brief ready to check against your own record before using it.

💡 Example

Input: "Harbor leads Summit 4–3 in the eighth, one out, runner on first. Summit's left-handed batter is due. Harbor's right-hander threw 22 pitches over the past two days; its left-hander has not pitched in three days. Should the left-hander enter now?"
Output: "If Harbor uses the left-hander now, the supplied three-day rest aligns with the stated left-handed matchup. Trade-off: no supplied split or current pitch count establishes a preferred move. Verify the current pitcher and bench options."

👤 Who it's for

Baseball broadcasters, beat writers, podcast producers, and serious fans who bring their own game notes and need a disciplined decision read.

What makes it different

- **Moment-specific input:** it analyzes one buyer-pasted inning, game state, and decision question rather than a pregame lineup, multi-game series, or completed-game log.
- **Decision-ready output:** it produces a decision card, conditional paths, coverage line, and verification queue rather than a score forecast or result prediction.
- **Honesty first:** it reasons from your pasted material and model knowledge only. It does not access a game feed, retrieve scores, lineups, odds, or availability updates; it does not place wagers or promise a result.
