The founder’s story · Daisuke Narita
“How can we
end suffering?”

I began practicing Vipassana around the age of 16. What started as a question about my own suffering grew into a question about suffering in the world.

In graduate school, I began asking how technology could help. Today, I’m building Anicca to work on that same question at a broader scale: the conditions and environments that shape our lives.

Now & what comes next

From individual feedback to shared conditions

Using synthetic populations and behavioral models to investigate which changes could help, then testing those ideas against real outcomes.

Manager · a transparent AI entity in development

One entity. No beneficiary app. Evidence in public.

Manager is the persistent AI decision-maker we are trying to bring into being. Its long-term design does not ask the people affected to open an app or send daily prompts; it acts through bounded, auditable changes and learns from their measured results. Our mission is to end suffering. This is an aspiration, not a claim that a universal model or AGI has been deployed.

Our research focuses on behavior: representing people and situations, predicting responses, evaluating persuasion and other behavior-changing interventions, and measuring what changes for individuals and groups. Simulation, executing an intervention, and improving human outcomes are separate evidence stages. The first finite capability is owned digital-state maintenance; it has not demonstrated behavior change or reduced suffering.

Population synthesis · measured output

441,570 households. 1,042,763 synthetic people.

Rhode Island public statistics and PUMS produced a household-preserving synthetic population. The output audit found no membership, duplicate-ID or copied-attribute mismatches. This checks population construction on that dataset; it does not validate behavior prediction, intervention effects or reduced suffering.

Behavior evaluation · open proposal

Build the benchmark before choosing a model.

We published the open EBA benchmark proposal and a runnable synthetic v0, alongside a map of 90 AI evaluations. The proposal separates prediction, action execution and human outcomes. Its human-behavior fidelity and causal welfare measures still need validation.

Existing model · synthetic-only test

Test existing models; keep the limits visible.

One Ampersand v0.1 experiment used ten fictional scenarios. It involved zero real participants and measured no real-world effects. We also recalculated its public aggregate scores. These are technical checks, not evidence that Ampersand or Manager changes human behavior.
